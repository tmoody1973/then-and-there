# 006 — Hard history is shown with a content note, not hidden

**Decision.** Racist ads, slurs and graphic crime coverage on historic pages are shown with a short content note and Editor-checked historical context. Teacher packs leave them out unless the teacher opts in.

**Why this came up.** Explore and teacher packs put these pages in front of students. The original plan said "present them with historical context; don't hide or decorate them," but didn't say how that works for a school audience.

**Options.**
1. **Show with a content note.** Faithful to the archive, prepares the reader, keeps teachers in control.
2. **Show everything as-is.** Most faithful; riskiest in a classroom.
3. **Hide by default.** Safest for schools; risks sanitizing the very history the project exists to surface, including how the Black press answered the papers that printed this material.

**What we chose and why.** Option 1, chosen by Tarik. It keeps the original "don't hide, don't decorate" rule and gives teachers the call for their own room.

**What we gave up.** Some build work: the pipeline needs a flag for this content, and the historical-context notes need the Editor's check. Some readers will still find notes inadequate or intrusive.

**How we'll know if this was right.** In a test with a teacher, flagged material is neither a surprise nor buried, and the context notes pass a historian's spot check.
