# 003 — One engine, two front doors: Play and Explore

**Decision.** Alongside the game, ship a thin "Story Finder" for students, teachers and history buffs: search, open the scan, cite. It uses the same verified clippings as the game.

**Why this came up.** Tarik asked whether the data could be useful beyond a game. The judging weights Impact at 30% ("who benefits, could people use it"), and a game alone is a weak answer to that.

**Options.**
1. **Play only.** Simplest; the story-finding stays invisible.
2. **Play + Explore** (two doors on one engine). The game brings people daily; the tool serves classes and researchers.
3. **Explore first, game as a feature.** Strongest utility, weakest hook.

**What we chose and why.** Option 2, chosen by Tarik from side-by-side mockups. It costs little extra because the pipeline already produces each clipping with its scan, clean text and citation. The Library provides ready-made APA, Chicago and MLA citations for every page.

**What we gave up.** Build time that could have polished the game. A full research platform (collections, sharing, a knowledge graph), deliberately kept out to stop Explore from eating the week.

**How we'll know if this was right.** A teacher or student can find a usable, cited primary source in under a minute in a hallway test, and judges mention the classroom use.
